Inventory & reorder tracker

A transparent replenishment tracker for three fictional products, with formulas, stated assumptions, and an escalation checklist.

Prepared for Joseph Berin’s portfolioDemonstration only
Fictional brand · Sample data

This project was created for portfolio demonstration. It is not paid client work, a live account audit, or evidence of business results.

Scenario & assumptions

Haven & Grain needs a daily stock check for three fictional SKUs. Average daily sales are fixed assumptions from a mock recent period. Lead time includes supplier preparation, transit, and warehouse check-in. The exercise uses a seven-day safety buffer and a 30-day additional coverage target.

Inbound stock is counted toward the inventory position only if it is confirmed to arrive before on-hand stock runs out. The rack’s 40 inbound units are assumed to arrive in ten days. Real replenishment should use actual demand variation, timing, order minimums, and storage constraints.

Priority notes

Two SKUs need a reorder review

HG-ORG-01: 16 days of on-hand cover versus a 21-day lead time. A normal replenishment may arrive after a stockout. Escalate the timing issue and explore approved expediting options.

HG-TRAY-03: 14 days of cover equals the lead time, leaving no safety buffer. Confirm availability and place an approved order promptly if the forecast holds.

HG-RACK-02: 45 days of on-hand cover, plus confirmed inbound stock. Keep monitoring rather than ordering from this snapshot.

Spreadsheet logic

Days left = available units ÷ average daily sales

Inventory position = available units + reliable inbound units

Reorder point = daily sales × (lead time + safety days)

Flag when inventory position ≤ reorder point

Suggested order = max(0, daily sales × (lead time + safety days + 30) − inventory position), only when flagged

Organizer example: 8 × (21 + 7) = 224-unit reorder point. The inventory position is 128. Suggested order: 8 × 58 − 128 = 336 units. This quantity does not solve the immediate lead-time gap.

Zero-sales handling: If daily sales are zero or missing, flag “Review demand” instead of dividing by zero or issuing an automatic reorder.

Daily review checklist

  1. Refresh available, reserved, stranded, and inbound stock from approved source reports.
  2. Check whether inbound quantities and arrival dates are still reliable.
  3. Review recent sales for promotions, seasonality, and unusual spikes.
  4. Confirm cash budget, pack sizes, order minimums, and supplier lead time.
  5. Send the stock-risk summary to the seller. Reorders require approval.
