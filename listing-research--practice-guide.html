Explaining the portfolio samples

A step-by-step guide to understanding and explaining these work samples before presenting them in applications or client interviews.

The reviewed product and website are real. These are independent, AI-assisted practice projects. OXO was not a client, and no sales or ranking improvement has been measured.

1. Review the Amazon listing sample

  1. Open the actual listing from the Sources section. Identify the title, selected variation, material, dimensions, and care instructions.
  2. Read the proposed title and five bullet points. Trace each factual claim to its source.
  3. Choose one bullet and rewrite it in your own words without adding any unsupported claims.
  4. Check whether the product remains understandable without the brand name. The item, intended use, and limitations should still be clear.

2. Work through the keyword workbook

  1. In Keyword map, review the Origin column. “Observed” refers to wording found in product sources. An “editorial candidate” is an idea that still needs validation.
  2. Filter the Decision column to Exclude. Explain why bamboo, teak, dishwasher-safe, and multi-item terms do not fit the reviewed product.
  3. In Copy draft, edit an amber draft cell. Observe the changes in character count and literal phrase coverage.
  4. Phrase coverage is not search volume and does not prove that a keyword is indexed. This project did not use a search-volume export.

3. Understand the SEO audit

Separate observed facts, interpretations, and recommendations. For example, a greeting in the H1 is an observed fact; a service-led heading is a recommendation. A claim that the greeting reduced rankings would not be an established fact.

A noindex directive on fictional samples may be intentional. Do not remove it from every page automatically. Decide separately whether new case studies should be available to search engines.

Interview questions

“Was OXO your client?”

No. This is an independent portfolio project using a publicly available listing and manufacturer information. I can walk you through the evidence, proposed changes and what would need validation before implementation.

“How did you choose the keywords?”

The map separates observed product language from editorial candidates. I prioritized relevance and removed terms that conflict with the material, care instructions or item count. Demand still needs validation with marketplace-specific data.

“Did your changes increase sales?”

The changes have not been published or tested. The deliverable is a researched proposal. With authorized account access, I would document a baseline and evaluate the approved changes using consistent reports or an eligible experiment.

“Did you use AI?”

Yes, this is an AI-assisted practice project. I use it to organize research and draft options. I verify the source facts and review the final decisions before using the work.

Only use the final sentence after personally reviewing the sources and decisions.

“Why not add every relevant phrase to the title?”

The title should identify the product clearly. A relevant phrase is not automatically a proven search opportunity, and literal phrase coverage is not an indexing test. I would avoid repetition and follow the applicable category rules.

Practice exercise

Record a two-minute explanation in your own words: what you reviewed, what you observed, what you proposed, and what remains unknown. Then answer these questions without reading the report:

  • Why does the audit not report “missing care instructions”?
  • Why was the more precise width omitted from the proposed title?
  • What is the difference between a keyword candidate and a measured search term?
  • Why is every audit item not necessarily an SEO ranking issue?
  • What account access or data would be needed to measure results?

Before publishing these samples, review the sources and practice explaining the decisions. Keep the portfolio descriptions aligned with the work you understand and can perform.
